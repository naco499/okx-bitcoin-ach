## What does it cost to buy Bitcoin with bank transfer?

The total cost can include several different components. They should not be treated as one single “Bitcoin fee.”

### Bank-transfer fee

OKX states that ACH purchases generally have no fee. However, your bank or payment provider may apply its own charge, and the exact amount shown at checkout is the final reference for your transaction.

### Trading fee

If you buy BTC through the spot market, OKX applies a maker or taker fee based on your account tier and the trading pair. A taker order executes against an existing order in the order book. A maker order adds liquidity to the order book and may receive a lower fee.

For smaller purchases, the standard fee tier is usually the relevant one. The current U.S. fee page lists the regular spot rate at **0.2000% maker** and **0.3500% taker**, subject to the applicable product, pair, account, and jurisdiction.

### Spread or quoted-price difference

The instant Buy interface can show a quote that differs from the last traded price on the order book. That difference is not always labeled in the same way as a trading fee. Compare the amount of BTC received and the total USD debited rather than looking only for a line called “commission.”

### Network withdrawal fee

If you later withdraw Bitcoin to an external wallet, the withdrawal may involve a separate network fee. That fee is separate from the cost of buying BTC and can change with network conditions and the withdrawal method.

### Taxes

Buying Bitcoin is not automatically the same as selling it for a gain. However, selling, swapping, or spending digital assets can create taxable events under U.S. tax rules. Keep records of purchase dates, amounts, transaction fees, cost basis, and later disposals. For tax decisions involving significant amounts, consult a qualified tax professional.

## Common problems when buying Bitcoin by bank transfer

### The bank-transfer option is missing

This usually points to one of the following:

- Your account is not fully verified.
- Your state or country is not eligible.
- Your bank connection has not completed.
- The selected asset or purchase route does not support that payment method.
- The feature is temporarily unavailable.
- Your account is subject to additional review.

Check the account verification section and the available payment methods shown in the actual Buy screen. OKX says ACH access depends on both verification and residency eligibility.

### The payment fails

Confirm that:

- The bank account name matches your OKX identity.
- The bank account has enough funds.
- Your bank has not blocked the transfer.
- The linked account is still active.
- You have not exceeded the displayed purchase limit.

Avoid repeatedly submitting the same failed payment. Multiple attempts can make it harder to identify whether the problem is with the bank, the account connection, or the purchase limit.

### The Bitcoin cannot be withdrawn

This can happen during a holding period after a bank-funded purchase. OKX notes that purchased crypto may be subject to a short period before it becomes eligible for withdrawal.

The safest approach is to treat the BTC as exchange-held until the withdrawal screen confirms that it is available for external transfer. If you plan to move it to a hardware wallet, first test a small withdrawal when permitted and verify the receiving address carefully.

### The final amount looks different from the expected amount

Bitcoin prices move continuously. The amount shown before confirmation can change if the quote expires or the order is filled at a different price. Fees, spread, partial fills, and the selected purchase route can also affect the final result.

For spot orders, OKX explains that the fee is calculated using the applicable fee rate and the amount of crypto bought or sold when the order fills. The exact amount can depend on how the order executes.
